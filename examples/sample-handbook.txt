FIELDSTONE — OPERATIONS HANDBOOK (SAMPLE DOCUMENT)

This is a fictional document included with RAG Inspector so you can try
document ingestion and chunking immediately. Any resemblance to a real
company is purely coincidental.

1. ABOUT THE APPROVAL PROCESS

Every request that changes a shared system, spends company budget, or
touches customer data must go through the approval process. A request is
anything a team member formally submits through the internal portal: a
purchase, an access change, a deployment, or a data export.

The approval process begins when a requester submits a complete form. A
form is complete when it includes a description, an estimated cost when
relevant, the responsible area, and a business reason. Incomplete forms
are returned to the requester with a short explanation.

The responsible area must review each request before it is forwarded to
the designated approver for final sign-off. The responsible area is the
team that owns the system, budget line, or data affected by the request.

The designated approver is assigned by role, never by name. When a person
changes teams, approvals follow the role, not the person.

2. WHO CAN APPROVE A REQUEST

Requests are approved by the manager of the responsible area. Requests
above the local budget threshold are escalated to the department head.
Requests that touch customer data are additionally reviewed by the
security lead before approval.

No one can approve their own request. If the only possible approver is
the requester, the request is automatically escalated one level up.

Delegated approvals are allowed only in writing, only for a limited
period, and only to a person with equivalent or higher responsibility.

3. RESPONSE TIMES

The target response time for a first review is two business days. The
target for final approval is five business days from submission.

Requests that sit untouched for more than five business days are flagged
as overdue and appear in the weekly operations report. Requesters can
nudge an approval once per request; repeated nudging is not encouraged
because the report already shows every overdue item.

Emergency requests follow the same rules but are approved through the
phone escalation path described in section six.

4. RESPONSIBILITIES

The requester is responsible for submitting complete information and for
reporting when an approved change is actually executed.

The responsible area is responsible for reviewing the technical and
business fit of the request, and for keeping documentation updated after
the change is made.

The approver is responsible for decisions, for record-keeping, and for
escalating anything that looks outside policy.

The operations team is responsible for the health of the approval system
itself, for the weekly report, and for auditing a random sample of closed
requests every month.

5. PROCEDURES AFTER APPROVAL

After approval, the responsible area executes the request and closes the
ticket in the tracking system. Closing the ticket requires a one-paragraph
summary of what changed and where it can be verified.

After approval, documentation must be updated within ten business days:
runbooks, access lists, and diagrams, as applicable.

After approval, the requester receives a notification with the final
decision, the approver's role, and the planned execution window.

If an approved request is abandoned, the requester must close it with the
reason "no longer needed" so that capacity planning stays accurate.

6. ESCALATION PATH

When a request is blocked, the requester raises a flag in the tracking
system. The responsible area has one business day to respond to a flag.

If the responsible area and the requester disagree, the department head
decides. The decision is recorded in the ticket with a short rationale.

If a request involves a suspected security incident, the normal process
is suspended and the incident runbook takes over immediately. Security
escalations are never blocked by budget limits or approval queues.

7. AUDIT AND EXCEPTIONS

All approvals are logged: who asked, who reviewed, who approved, when,
and what changed. The log is immutable.

Exceptions to the approval process are rare, time-boxed, and require
department head sign-off. An exception that is renewed twice is a signal
that the policy itself should change.

8. SMALL PRINT

Requests below the petty threshold still need a reviewer but not an
approver. Access reviews happen quarterly regardless of how an access
grant was approved. This handbook is reviewed every six months, and
changes to the approval process are announced at least two weeks before
they take effect.
